TPP-GAZE ASD/TD - LIMITATIONS

1. No participant IDs in Saliency4ASD.
   The processed data have no per-observer identifiers across images, so participant-level generalization
   cannot be established for that dataset. Uncertainty there covers images only, and conditioning uses the
   group label alone. Participant-level statistics were possible only on Gaze4ASD.

2. Gaze4ASD display mapping is strongly supported but partly inferred.
   Fit-to-height scaling and horizontal centring were not directly confirmed by the original presentation
   script, because the draw rectangle is not in the dataset. Position NLL, absolute spatial metrics and
   MultiMatch depend on this mapping. The original native-size mapping was contradicted by the data, and
   its results are superseded.

3. Gaze4ASD Policy A clips off-image fixations to image boundaries.
   This affects 3.52% of fixations (x 1.53%, y 2.24%) and piles points on image edges. Policy B, which removes
   them instead, was run as a sensitivity analysis on the V2 evaluation. The Phase 1 vs Phase 2 and tau=1
   comparisons use Policy A only.

4. External stimulus selection may not be fully independent.
   Gaze4ASD stimulus selection may have used Saliency4ASD. Participants and images are separate, but the
   selection process may not be. Stimuli were chosen for ASD/TD gaze differences and are mostly faces, so
   effects may be inflated, and predictability may be higher than for general images.

5. Conditioning does not generalize.
   Conditioning improves in-domain likelihood slightly (+0.0073 nats/event, no confidence interval computed) but
   does not improve overall corrected external likelihood (-0.0172 nats/event against Phase 2). It is a 1,024-
   parameter additive embedding. The cause of the worse external TD fit has not been tested.

6. Generated fixation counts and total viewing time remain too high.
   At tau=1, generated count is 10.24 (ASD) and 9.62 (TD) against real 6.25 and 6.41; total fixation time is 2.79 and
   2.77 s against 1.99 and 2.07 s. The sampler has no stopping model and fills the 3 s window. Excess remains within
   the real viewing window (+1.23 and +1.00). Generated durations are also too short.

7. Some generated group differences have the wrong direction.
   Direction concentration, total fixation time and fixation count (the last not significant in real data) are
   wrong at both tau=1 and tau=2. Even correct contrasts are too small, and per-image correspondence is weak.

8. Revisit-rate magnitude is under-reproduced.
   Revisit rate is the largest real ASD-vs-TD effect (g = -1.04), but the generated difference is only 29% of real at
   tau=1 (15% at tau=2).

9. Temperature changes sampling behaviour but does not change NLL.
   tau=1 improves behavioural measures only. It does not improve the model. It was selected on validation images,
   which also selected the Phase 2 checkpoint. Only tau=1 and 2 were compared. The TD revisit improvement reverses
   within the real viewing window. Direction concentration is slightly worse at tau=1. The official TPP-Gaze
   config uses tau=2.

10. The original TPP-Gaze Transformer has a batch-attention implementation quirk.
    The encoder has batch_first=False but receives (batch, length, dim) tensors, so attention mixes across the batch,
    and results depend on batch composition. Causal time ordering comes from the LSTM. This is in the released model
    and was reproduced, not fixed. Shuffling batches changed overall Gaze4ASD NLL by about 0.0001 or less. Generation
    uses single-image, single-condition batches of 20.

11. Statistical tests and generation comparisons are exploratory.
    They are not preregistered confirmatory evidence. Four temperature tests were prespecified before generation, but
    earlier tau=2 results on Gaze4ASD had already been seen. Holm correction applies within each family only. Effect
    sizes such as g and dz are not on the same scale, and generated p-values depend on the number of samples.

12. No clinical diagnosis or clinical validity is established.
    These are group-level computational results. They are not a diagnostic, screening or individual-level tool.

Additional notes
- Groups are unbalanced on Gaze4ASD (33 ASD vs 133 TD participants), and age was not adjusted (mean 5.04 vs 4.54 years).
- Phase 1 was not run at tau=1, so the Phase 1 vs Phase 2 behavioural comparison applies to tau=2 only.
- MultiMatch is our own implementation, with a high ceiling and limited discrimination. Higher scores are not
  automatically more realistic, because tighter sampling raises similarity.
- Generated data have no participants, so generated uncertainty covers only images and seeds.
- The final Phase 2 configuration (embedding only, lr 1e-4) followed an earlier full-base attempt rejected on validation.
